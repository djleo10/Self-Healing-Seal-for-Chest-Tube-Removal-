# Self-Healing Seal for Chest Tube Removal

A concept for an internal, self-healing chest-tube attachment that seals the insertion site the moment the tube is withdrawn, to reduce air leaks and pneumothorax after thoracic surgery.

*Team 2: Dheemant Jallepalli, Jared Scott, Victor Chiu, Tushar Nayak, Pranav Kini, Defne Kudug — Surgery for Engineers, Carnegie Mellon University (2025)*

## The clinical problem

- A **pneumothorax** happens when air enters the space between the lung and chest wall and the lung partly or fully collapses.
- Removing a chest tube after lung surgery can let air back in. Post-operative air leaks are one of the most frequent complications after lung resection; in one study of 497 patients, 81 developed a pneumothorax after their chest tube was removed.
- Recurrence means more discomfort, longer hospital stays and extra procedures.

## Design requirements

**During surgery:** fits standard 20–40 French chest tubes, doesn't change insertion technique, biocompatible (ISO 10993), sterile (ISO 11135), radiopaque for X-ray, doesn't block drainage, fixes securely.

**After surgery:** seals automatically on withdrawal with no extra clinician step, doesn't raise removal force, stays airtight for 24–48 h, works with suction drainage systems, easy to use, comes in several sizes, disposes as standard medical waste, and has at least a two-year shelf life.

## Proposed solution

An **internal self-healing chest-tube attachment** that closes the tract behind the tube as it is pulled out.
